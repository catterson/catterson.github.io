Readings for Next Week (and Quiz 2)

  1. The Goldacre (2010) Chapter from “Why Clever People Believe Stupid Things”. Some of these biases can be confusing - okay to use other sources or AI tools to help you understand, but let us know if you do!

  2. Research for Fun and Profit. A summary of scientific articles - where researchers read (and write) about the research studies. Review the publication process, types of articles, the peer review process, and different methods of evaluating a research article.

  3. Scan the following example of peer-review in practice. You don’t need to read everything, but wanted to give y’all a flavor of what peer-review actually looks like in practice.

    • Here are my responses to peer-review from an article I wrote that ultimately got accepted.

    • Here is an editor’s letter in response to an article I wrote that got rejected. The original paper was a bit of a hot mess, but I think some of their critiques also reflect some deeper problems in the field that have led to the replication crisis (e.g., “evidence from Study 1 (with 4 samples completing just 2 items) is described as “compelling” when for some but not all samples the 2 items are positively correlated”).

  4. Optional Readings : Not required, but thought these might be of interest for students who were interested!

    • More on the Mechanisms of Scientific Publishing.

      • An Overview of the Peer-Review processes from an academic publisher. A few tabs on the videos you can click through to see what is required and involved.

      • Scientific Journals and Articles. A really nicely written (I think) review of scientific articles, and how to read them.

    • More on the Business of Scientific Publishing.

      • A much longer interview with Alexandra Elbakyan, the founder of Sci-Hub, on scientific communism. Here’s a PDF in case you get pay-walled.

      • A much longer discussion of the history of academic publishing as a big business.

Week 2 Vision Board Questions.

YOUR POST : Use the readings to answer the following questions. Please submit answers to the Vision Board (Week 2 Tab).

  1. Using your research question (from Week 1, or feel free to revise!), define a linear model for the research question (and theory) that you might be interested to explore as a researcher. Then, define a null and alternative hypothesis for this question.

  2. Summarize one bias from the Goldacre reading in your own words. And then think of a real-life example of where this bias might apply. Ask AI (or a real-life human friend) to explain this cognitive bias and give an example in less than 50 words. Paste the response here (and let us know which tool you used). Did AI seem to get this right?

  3. Share a screenshot or advertisement or social media post that seems trying to influence your belief in some way (or, if you’re feeling creative…make up your own advertisement or social media post to influence others). Identify at least one of the cognitive biases that might influence your (or others’) beliefs about this post. Make sure you describe how that bias is relevant to the image. (e.g., “in this advertisement, our professor who has authority over our grades is telling us to DRINK TAP WATER.”)

  4. We all have questions. What is something you learned, or a question you had, about academic journals or the peer-review process?

  5. REPLY TO ANOTHER STUDENT’S POST : Choose one! Make sure to add your name and say “hi”.

  • Add some variables to the student’s linear model that might be related to the DV.

  • Describe how another cognitive biases might be relevant to the shared advertisement, or think about how the advertisement could add other cognitive biases (e.g., the advertisement of the FRESH TAP WATER could sparkle more to be more alluring / memorable and increase AVAILABILITY BIAS.)
